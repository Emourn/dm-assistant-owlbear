## 🧙 PLAYER 1 — Human Wizard

### Who You Are

You are the eldest, legitimate son of **Lord Dagult Neverember**, Lord Protector of Neverwinter. Your father loves you — genuinely. He isn't the warmest man in the world, but he cared enough to give you the best education money and influence could buy. He pulled strings to have you accepted as a personal student of **Alustriel Silverhand**, one of the most powerful archmages alive, in the city of **Silverymoon**.

You spent a significant portion of your youth there, studying the arcane arts under Alustriel's direct tutelage. She was genuinely pleased to observe that you possessed not only an innate talent for magic, but a brilliant, inquisitive mind. Your lack of a social filter when dealing with nobility was perhaps less than ideal, but in the relentless pursuit of arcane knowledge, that same trait proved invaluable.

While in Silverymoon, you had a roommate named **Hanniel Woodenfall**, and to this day you still keep in touch. It is an unexpected friendship, but one you welcome warmly — a refreshing contrast to the kinds of relationships you are used to within your father’s estate.

Your deepest fear is, ironically, your own reality. You fear that your life, the loneliness you feel from your disconnection with others, the sense that you are more machine than man... all of it might be nothing more than an illusion. A cruel joke or a game in which you have been forced to play a part for reasons you cannot understand. You despise the crude and childish game that is "power," but when it comes to *knowledge*, you will risk almost anything.

### The Scar

Driven by your unfiltered curiosity, while exploring Alustriel's personal vault, you found something you shouldn't have: a **small, ancient artifact** — clearly Netherese in origin. It pulsated with an energy unlike any magic you'd ever studied. Something about it called to you.

When you touched it, it **detonated silently**. Not fire, not force — a white-hot pulse that passed through your body and left behind a **scar** (you decide where it is and what it looks like). The artifact went completely dead. Inert. A lifeless stone. You've kept it ever since.

Alustriel examined you afterward and told you to "be careful." She didn't explain further.

The scar doesn't hurt. It tingles sometimes, near strong magic, almost like static. You've never been able to figure out what the artifact was, what it did to you, or why Alustriel won't talk about it.

### The True Mother & The Guilt

Your biological mother died during childbirth; your only memories of her are borrowed from your father and a portrait. The woman you have truly called "mother" all your life is a servant: **Gwendolyn Autumnbloom**. She has spent her life in service to your family. She taught you nobility, etiquette, and the subtle customs no one else bothered with. You are clumsy at expressing yourself, but you love her deeply.

You have made mistakes, but you absolutely refuse to be manipulated. You have seen how these games unfold among the nobility. You've suffered betrayals, but the greatest comes from your father: he always appears open to treating your bastard brother differently, yet never truly changes. 

This causes you immense guilt. You feel you committed the greatest betrayal by "stealing" a piece of your brother's past so that you could succeed. You genuinely wish to set things right for him in whatever way you can... though perhaps that ship sailed long ago.

### What You Know

- You love your father, but despise his manipulative political games.
- **Gwendolyn Autumnbloom**, a servant, is the true mother figure in your life. You love her deeply.
- You have a younger bastard brother. You feel immense guilt over "stealing" his past, and you desperately want to set things right for him.
- You maintain a refreshing correspondence with your old Silverymoon roommate, **Hanniel Woodenfall**.
- You trained under Alustriel Silverhand. She is brilliant and praised your unfiltered academic mind, but she keeps secrets.
- You carry a dead Netherese artifact and a scar you can't explain. You value pure *knowledge* over power, and you will pursue the truth relentlessly.
- Your deepest fear is that reality itself is a cruel, illusory game.

### What You Don't Know

You know there's more to the scar than Alustriel is telling you. You don't know what.

## 🌑 PLAYER 2 — Cassius Neverember (Human Warlock)

### Who You Are

You are the younger, illegitimate son of **Lord Dagult Neverember**. You grew up in the Neverember household alongside your brother, but you were always aware of your place: the complication. The mistake. Your father housed you, fed you, acknowledged you — but never with the warmth he showed your brother.

You are not bitter. You are *sharp*. You learned early that the world doesn't hand you things, so you take them. You're a scoundrel with a good heart, even if you'd never admit it.

Your **mother died** when you were young. She was a wizard — self-taught, not famous, but real. She left behind a **spellbook**. You kept it as a keepsake for years but never opened it. It felt too personal. Too sacred.

### The Candlekeep Sacrifice

Your brother needed to get into **Candlekeep** — the greatest library in the world — to research his scar. Entry requires a donation of a book with arcane significance. You had nothing to give except your mother's spellbook.

So you gave it. For him.

They found nothing useful. And Candlekeep doesn't return donations. Your mother's spellbook — the only thing you had of hers — is gone. Somewhere in those endless stacks, unread, untouched.

### The Pact

One day, you got yourself into real trouble. The kind that ends with you bleeding out in an alley in Neverwinter. You ran. You hid. And for the first time in your life, alone and terrified, you called out for help.

Something answered. A **warm voice**. A woman who felt like wildflowers and old libraries. She said she could help. She said she'd keep you safe. And one day, she'd need your help in return.

You said yes. The pact was sealed. You don't know her real name. You call her your patron, and her power flows through you.

### Your Mother's Secret

Your mother's death has always felt... wrong. It wasn't a sudden illness or a tragic accident. She was healthy, and then she wasn't. But more than that, there was an air of paranoia about her in the final months. She was terrified of something. You've mostly stopped asking questions, because the answers never come.

### The Candlekeep Sacrifice

Your brother needed to get into **Candlekeep** — the greatest library in the world — to research his arcane scar. Entry requires a donation of a book with immense arcane significance. Your brother (despite your father's wealth) couldn't source one in time. You had nothing to give except your mother's spellbook.

So you gave it. For him.

They found nothing useful about the scar. And Candlekeep doesn't return donations. Your mother's spellbook — the only thing you had of hers, the only record of what she might have been studying before she died — is gone. Somewhere in those endless, heavily guarded stacks, unread and untouched.

### The Pact

Years later, you got yourself into real trouble. A group of ruthless thugs cornered you in a Neverwinter alley. They didn't feel like normal thugs; they were silent, efficient, and they almost completely ignored your purse. They were there to end you.

Bleeding out, you ran, hid, and for the first time in your life, alone and terrified, you called out for help. 

Something answered. A **warm voice**. A woman who felt like wildflowers and old libraries. She said she could help. She said she'd keep you safe. And one day, she'd need your help in return.

You said yes. The thugs simply collapsed, unconscious. 

The pact was sealed. You don't know her real name. You call her your patron, and her power flows through you. 

### What You Know

- You love your brother, despite his flaws and the vast differences in how your father treats you.
- Your father doesn't think much of you. It stings, but you've made peace with it. Mostly.
- You made a pact with a mysterious, warm presence who saved your life from unnatural thugs. You owe her a debt.
- Your mother didn't die naturally. She was hunting for something, or being hunted.
- Your mother's spellbook—the only clue to her death—is locked in Candlekeep. You sacrificed it unread for your brother.

### What You Don't Know

You don't know who your patron really is, or why she chose to save you. You don't know who sent those thugs to kill you in the alley. You don't know what impossible secrets are hidden inside your mother's spellbook in Candlekeep.

## ⚔️ PLAYER 3 — Tiefling Paladin

### Who You Are

You were not always a tiefling.

Long ago, you were one of the Shadar‑kai, servants of the enigmatic Raven Queen, the sovereign of memory, fate, and death within the endless gloom of the Shadowfell. Like others of your kind, you served her faithfully, believing utterly in her divine purpose.

Your duty was sacred and solemn. You gathered the lingering memories, emotions, and echoes of the dead—those fragments that cling to souls as they pass beyond mortality. These you catalogued and delivered to the Raven Queen’s domain, the legendary Fortress of Memories, where every life ever lived becomes part of an eternal archive.

You loved her as only a devoted servant can love a god—without doubt, without hesitation.

But devotion is fragile when confronted by life itself.

While carrying out your duties, you met someone who did not belong to the Shadar-kai. He was still an elf, but unlike those who served the Raven Queen. His name was Vazhir.

Vazhir was gentle, warm in a way the Shadowfell rarely allows. He treasured life and every fleeting moment it contained. When he looked at you, it was not with the cold acceptance of death that defined your people, but with kindness and wonder. His smile reached places within your soul that had long been silent.

You began meeting in secret. 

What began as curiosity became affection, and affection became love. Hidden from the eyes of the Raven Queen’s court, the two of you shared stolen moments—quiet hours that felt impossibly bright in a realm of twilight.

Then your secret gave rise to something neither of you expected.

You were with child.

As life began to grow within you, something inside you changed. The stories of the souls you collected no longer felt distant or abstract. Each life began to matter. Every death felt heavier. You imagined the future—your child, Vazhir, a life that might exist beyond endless service to death.

For the first time, you understood what it meant to hope.

Then tragedy came.

Only weeks after you realized the life growing within you, your child died before ever drawing its first breath beneath the moon. No cry marked its birth. No story would ever be written for it.

The grief that followed shattered you.

Before, death had simply been what it was: inevitable, impartial, a quiet conclusion to every story. That was the doctrine you had always lived by.

But now death hurt.

Now death mattered.

Vazhir mourned beside you, though even his strength could not shield either of you from the sorrow. For the first time in your existence, you wept not for a memory you collected—but for a life that should have been.

And in that grief, you failed your queen.

The Raven Queen demands absolute impartiality from her servants. Death must be observed, not mourned. Recorded, not judged.

Your sorrow was weakness.

Your compassion, defiance.

And so she punished you.

You were cast into a prison of endless darkness, trapped within a cage of shadow where time stretched beyond meaning. In that abyss you heard the echoes of unborn souls crying endlessly into the void—again and again, without end. It was a punishment meant to scour compassion from your soul, to burn away the empathy that had taken root within you.

But it did not work.

The suffering did not erase your grief.

It only made you understand it more deeply.

When you were finally released, ages might have passed—or perhaps only years. Time means little in the Shadowfell. Weak and broken, you returned to the place where Vazhir had once waited for you.

What you found there was not the man you loved.

Only the faded remains of what he had been.

He had waited for you until death claimed him. His life had burned away slowly in hope that you would return.

Your love had ended not with a farewell, but with silence and dust.

The Raven Queen saw that your compassion remained unchanged. You had not learned the lesson she intended.

So she cast you out.

Your punishment became exile.

She stripped away your Shadar-kai nature and severed your place among her servants. Your soul was bound to infernal power and reshaped into something alien to what you had once been. Your flesh burned and twisted, reforming into that of a Tiefling—a being whose appearance carried the unmistakable mark of fiendish blood.

It was not inheritance.

It was a curse.

A permanent mark of divine rejection carved into your body.

The servant who once loved her goddess now carried the form of something the Raven Queen would never claim again.

You wandered the worlds after that—through kingdoms and roads and cities—bearing the weight of an unending life. You watched people live, love, struggle, and die. Each life burned briefly, but brightly.

And you remembered them.

Always remembering.

The world beyond the Shadowfell taught you something your old existence never had: death is not only an ending. It is part of transformation. Nature destroys, but it also renews and nourishes.

From those experiences you formed a vow of your own—a personal oath shaped by everything you had lost.

**Every soul, living or dead, deserves respect.**

**Every life deserves to be mourned.**

**No story should be reduced to a cold entry in an archive of memories.**

### The Church of Jergal & The Tattoos

Eventually, your path led you to the church of Jergal, the ancient Lord of the End of Everything. Unlike the Raven Queen’s servants, Jergal’s followers treated death with solemn reverence rather than cold detachment. It felt like a place where your beliefs might finally belong.

You served there for years.

But over time, something began to feel wrong.

Hidden in the lower halls of the temple, you noticed symbols that should not have been there. Strange markings. Tattoos on the hands of the oldest clerics. Prayers whispered in secret to something that was not Jergal.

And you recognized the signs.

An eye.

A hand.

The ancient iconography of Vecna.

The church had been infiltrated—or perhaps corrupted from within long ago. You investigated quietly until your suspicions became certainty.

Then you left.

Now you carry that knowledge with you. You can recognize those symbols anywhere. You know what they mean, even if you still do not fully understand the scale of what you uncovered.

### The Fortune-Teller 

Eventually your wandering brought you to the city of Neverwinter.

There, shortly after your arrival, you visited a fortune-teller. She was an unsettling old woman who appeared in the city for a single night and vanished the next day without leaving a trace.

When she looked at you, her eyes widened with recognition.

Then she said only this:

*"You will hold the eye of a god in your hand.*
*You will be asked to return it.*
*If you do, everything ends.*
*If you refuse, everything changes.*
*Either way… you will lose something you love."*

You have carried those words ever since. Days, months—perhaps longer. You tell yourself it was nonsense. But you are not certain.

### Anchors in Neverwinter

In Neverwinter you have made a few new companions. In a tavern you befriended **Brante**, an older tiefling with a booming laugh and a welcoming smile who owns the establishment, and **Eloys**, a red-haired dwarf woman capable of drinking nearly anyone under the table. When you had nowhere to stay, they offered you shelter without questions.

Most people from your past are gone now. Those who remain would not recognize you in your new form.

As for being a tiefling—your feelings are complicated. But you have come to understand something important.

Flesh is only a shell. The soul beneath it is what matters.

And one day, like every other soul that has ever lived, yours will also reach its end.

Until then, you will remember.

## 🌿 PLAYER 4 — Elf Druid

### Who You Are

You remember nothing of the life you once lived. You do not know how you came to possess the body you now inhabit—the age you appear to be, the height you carry, the face you see reflected in still water. You do not know what shaped your personality, nor whether you have always been the quiet, thoughtful soul you seem to be now. Your days are filled with a strange melancholy, a persistent nostalgia for something you cannot name. It is the feeling of remembering something that is not quite there, like a dream that dissolves the moment one tries to grasp it.

What you do know is that something is missing.

You feel it constantly—a hollow place within you, an absence that refuses to be ignored. Sometimes it feels as though someone is waiting for you. At other times it feels like a forgotten promise, or a path you once walked but can no longer see. Whatever it is, it has not left you. Until clarity returns, you can do little more than live and hope that one day the truth will find its way back to you.

And yet, buried deep beneath the emptiness, there remains a fragment of memory—not of events, but of feeling. Once, you had been whole. Once, you had carried a purpose you longed to fulfill with every fiber of your being. That purpose was taken from you for reasons you cannot understand. The loss stirs something painful inside you, something that burns quietly in your chest. Sometimes you wonder if it is guilt, or regret—perhaps the consequence of a choice never made, or a moment that passed when you should have acted.

### The Grove & Nhyma

You awoke weak and disoriented on the edge of a druidic grove, your body exhausted and your mind fractured. The druids of the circle who lived there took you in without hesitation. They gave you shelter, tended to your wounds, and allowed you to remain among them while you struggled to understand the strange emptiness within your mind.

Your name came from the only clue you carried with you: a delicate piece of embroidery stitched into the worn clothing you wore when you arrived. The garments were little more than tattered cloth by that point, but the name stitched into them remained legible.

**Seelie.**

It was the only thing you had left of your past. The sound of the name stirred warmth within you, a faint sense of comfort—as though it belonged to you. And yet, there was something else beneath the feeling. A quiet unease, a strange instinct that the name should not be spoken too freely, nor heard by just anyone.

Within the grove, you found an unexpected sense of peace. At its heart stood an ancient tree older than any of the druids who tended it, its vast roots coiling around a sacred stone the circle protected with quiet reverence. Something about the place felt familiar to you, though you could not explain why. Drawn to its stillness, you often spent long hours resting beneath the tree’s branches.

Without memories of your own, you discovered that curiosity filled the empty spaces where experience should have been. You listened eagerly to the stories of others. Among the druids, one in particular became deeply important to you: **Nhyma**, one of the circle’s leaders.

Nhyma possessed a patience and composure that few could match. She helped you search for fragments of identity within the habits you displayed, the instincts you carried, and the strange drawings you produced almost unconsciously. Elegant, composed, and quietly maternal, Nhyma became a grounding presence in your uncertain life—a steady light guiding you forward through unfamiliar darkness.

### The Dream Journal

From the moment you arrived, you had begun sketching images—scenes that came to you in dreams or flashes of thought. Some were clear and vivid, while others were incomplete or fragmented. They depicted places you had never seen, landscapes that stirred unease or anticipation within you. 

Rather than dismiss them, Nhyma encouraged the habit. She taught you to look beyond the surface of what you drew, to search for meaning in the shapes and symbols. The journals you have filled feel important. Important enough that you cannot bring yourself to stop recording them.

### The Girl in the Shadows

There is one shadowed memory that lingers more strongly than the others.

A girl.

You grew up together, inseparable. Perhaps you had even been raised for the same purpose, though the details remain frustratingly unclear. If you were thoughtful and measured, the other girl was your opposite—bold, impulsive, and fiercely alive. Together you balanced one another perfectly. Strategy and action. Light and shadow.

The girl’s name may have been **Thea**. Or perhaps **Eleine**.

Whatever the truth, the memory of her carries both warmth and sorrow. Somewhere within that lost past lies the bitter taste of betrayal—or perhaps simply the realization that you had once been manipulated by forces you did not fully understand.

You are terrified of losing the life you have begun to rebuild. If the fragile peace you have found within the grove were taken from you, the weight of it might be unbearable. Even so, you continue forward. Because something is waiting for you. And one day, you intend to find it.

### What You Know

- You awoke with no memories, possessing only tattered clothes with the name **Seelie** embroidered on them.
- You were taken in by a druidic circle, forming a deep, maternal bond with a leader named **Nhyma**.
- You compulsively sketch vivid, strange dreams in journals.
- You have a strong, lingering memory of a girl you grew up with—bold and impulsive—named **Thea** or **Eleine**, associated with a feeling of betrayal or manipulation.
- You fear losing your new life in the grove more than anything, but you deeply desire to understand your past.

### What You Don't Know

You don't know what your true purpose was. You don't know how you got to the grove. You don't know the full story of Thea/Eleine's betrayal. You don't know what the sacred stone beneath the ancient tree truly is.

## 🐉 PLAYER 5 — Lasair (Dragonborn Wild Magic Sorcerer)

### Who You Are

You were not born to greatness. You were not chosen by prophecy. You were stolen.

Before you ever hatched, your egg was taken in **Athkatla**. You don't know who took you, or why. What you know — what you've pieced together over years of bitter, obsessive investigation — is that dark-robed figures transported your egg alongside something they shouldn't have been carrying. A stone. Black, glassy, humming with a frequency that had no business existing in the natural world. They kept you too close to it for too long, and whatever that stone was, it got inside you before you ever drew breath.

You were lost during a raid on the smugglers' operation. A kind couple found you — or rather, found your egg — and raised you as their own. They were not wealthy. They were not powerful. They were good. They gave you a name. They gave you a home. They loved you without condition, without agenda, and without ever once making you feel like you were something *other*.

Years later, the people who stole you came back to collect their property.

Your parents — your *real* parents, the ones who chose you — died so that you could run. You ran. You survived. You have never forgiven yourself for surviving and you have never stopped hating the people who made that choice necessary.

### Neverwinter

You ended up in **Neverwinter**. You became an investigator — not because you wanted justice in some abstract, noble sense, but because you wanted to find the people who murdered your parents and make them answer for it. Along the way, you partnered with **Kevori** and **Delvin Fearnehart**, and the three of you became something you hadn't expected: a family.

You aren't a mystic. You aren't a scholar. You're a boots-on-the-ground, knock-on-doors, read-the-ledger, follow-the-money kind of investigator. Kevori is sharp and practical. Delvin is warm and steady. Together, the three of you work. You trust them with your life — and more importantly, they keep you sane.

Because you need keeping sane.

### The Curse

Whatever that stone did to you in the egg, it broke something fundamental. Your magic doesn't work the way magic is supposed to work. It's violent. Unpredictable. It lashes out in surges you can barely control — **Wild Magic**, the scholars call it, as if giving it a name makes it less terrifying.

But the Wild Magic is the least of it.

**The Time Slips.** Your mind remembers too much. Not in the gentle, fading way that memory normally works — your mind *replays* things. When your magic surges, you don't just remember your parents dying. You are *there*. You hear the door splinter. You smell the smoke. You feel the cold of the floor under your feet as you run. It happens in a flash — half a second of real time — but inside your head, you live the entire moment again. Sometimes you perceive fragments of things that haven't happened yet. Sometimes you see ghosts — echoes of people recently dead, walking beside you as if they don't know they're gone.

Your mind is painfully, agonizingly crowded. It is never quiet.

**The Obsidian.** The surges are doing something to your body. Every time you push your magic too hard, patches of your natural scales darken, harden, and crystallize into black, glassy obsidian — the exact same material as the stone that irradiated your egg. It started small. A chip on your knuckle. A dark patch behind your ear. It's spreading. You don't know what happens when it finishes, and you don't want to find out.

When the noise gets too loud — when the time slips pull you under and the obsidian itches beneath your scales — it isn't meditation that brings you back. It's Kevori complaining about the weather. It's Delvin talking about what he's making for dinner. It's the messy, mundane, unglamorous texture of mortal life. You cling to it the way a drowning man clings to driftwood.

### The Nightmares

You dream of a stone. Massive. Black. Screaming.

Not a metaphor. It literally screams — thousands of voices crying out in agony beneath the surface, broadcasting on a frequency that only you seem able to hear. You wake drenched in sweat, the obsidian patches on your body warm to the touch.

The people who stole you are still out there. They called you their property. And you have a sickening, gut-level certainty that whatever they did to you, they did on purpose.

### What You Know

- You were stolen as an egg in Athkatla. The people who took you exposed you to a strange, black stone that permanently scarred your magic.
- Your adoptive parents raised you with love. They were murdered by the same people who stole you, so that you could escape.
- You are an investigator in Neverwinter, anchored by your partners **Kevori** and **Delvin Fearnehart**. They are your sanity.
- Your Wild Magic causes agonizing "Time Slips" — you relive your worst memories as if they are happening right now.
- Your scales are slowly turning into black obsidian. It is visibly getting worse.
- You dream of a massive screaming stone that no one else can hear.

### What You Don't Know

You don't know who the people that stole you really are, or what they worship. You don't know what the black stone was, or why it screams in your dreams. You don't know why your magic feels less like spellcasting and more like static. And you don't know if the obsidian spreading across your body can be stopped.

## ⚙️ PLAYER 6 — Coronal (Elf Cartographer Artificer)

### Who You Are

You were born on **Evermeet**, and you should have been grateful.

The sanctuary isle. The jewel of the elven race. A place most elves will never see, let alone call home. Its towers were raised by hands that have long turned to dust. Its wards were woven by the greatest mages your people have ever produced. Every garden, every bridge, every mosaic on every wall — all of it was finished long, long before you drew your first breath.

Evermeet doesn't need architects. It doesn't need cartographers. It doesn't need someone who looks at a wall and instinctively thinks about how it could be stronger, or a bridge and wonders why no one built it wider. You grew up surrounded by immortal beauty and felt, for most of your life, like a stonemason born into a world made entirely of glass.

You weren't idle. You studied. You sketched. You proposed designs — improvements to existing structures, new fortifications, charts of the coastlines and the currents beyond the island's reach. Your elders praised your diligence the way one praises a child's painting: warmly, and with no intention of hanging it on the wall.

What you actually wanted was simple. You wanted to *leave*. To see the world beyond the wards. To chart shorelines no elf had walked in centuries, to survey the ruins of Myth Drannor, to map the old roads the People abandoned when they retreated to this island. You wanted to *build* something — not admire what others had built a thousand years ago.

Your elders called this restlessness. They called it youth. Eventually, one of them called it vanity.

Maybe it was.

### The Crime of Evermeet

Your frustration attracted something.

It began as dreams — vivid, architectural dreams. A voice, old and feminine, who spoke to you not as a wayward child, but as an equal. She called you gifted. She said your elders were fools for keeping you leashed to an island that had outgrown the need for your talents. She understood your hunger because she had seen it before — in the great elven artificers of old, the ones who built the Mythal of Myth Drannor. The ones who *dared*.

She said she could show you how Evermeet's ancient wards actually worked — not the sanitized theory your tutors taught, but the true architecture. The living bones of the magic that held the island together. If you could understand the wards, you could chart the flow of magical energy across the land itself. You could map the invisible.

You should have questioned why a mysterious voice was feeding you secrets in the dark. You didn't. You were too hungry to hear someone say *yes*.

Following her guidance, you built a device. Small. Elegant. A resonating instrument designed to interact with the harmonic frequencies of the island's protective wards — to read them, to chart them, to finally *understand* the architecture everyone else simply took for granted.

You activated it in one of Evermeet's sacred scrying chambers.

For a few glorious seconds, it worked. You could feel the wards like a living thing — the hum of ancient magic flowing through stone and sea and sky. You'd been right. The architecture was there, hidden, waiting to be read by someone who actually bothered to look.

Then the device overloaded. It didn't just read the wards. It *tore* them.

A breach opened. Shadowfell entities — cold, screaming things — poured through the gap and corrupted an ancient grove before the island's high mages sealed the rift. No one died, but the damage was real. Sacred ground, polluted. By *your* hand.

### The Exile & The Scar

The High Council's judgment was swift. They shattered your device. They stripped you of your societal names — every title, every lineage marker, every thread connecting you to your people. And they cast you out to the mainland. You were no longer of Evermeet.

The exile hurt. But it wasn't what broke you.

What broke you came later. Alone, replaying every detail, the pieces finally fell into place. The "muse" hadn't been impressed by you. She'd *needed* someone arrogant enough to weaken the wards — to test whether a breach could be forced from the inside. Your elegant device was a hag's experiment, and you were the fool who built it for her while she applauded your brilliance.

Everything you'd told yourself — that you were the exception, the one elf with enough vision to see what others couldn't — had been someone else's script. Your moment of genius was a puppet show.

From the wreckage of that humiliation, you made a vow. Not a noble one. A desperate, ugly one: **you would never be someone else's tool again.** You would never be vulnerable. You would never let flattery, ambition, or trust make you blind a second time.

### Rebuilding in the Shadows (Athkatla)

Stripped of name and homeland, you drifted to **Athkatla** — the merchant capital of Amn. A city of coin, intrigue, and a deeply practical ban on unlicensed magic enforced by the Cowled Wizards. It was the last place anyone would look for a disgraced elven artificer, and that suited you perfectly.

You survived. Slowly, you rebuilt. You took work as a surveyor, a draftsman, a consultant for wealthy merchants who needed structures assessed or property mapped. Honest, anonymous work. Work that didn't require trust.

Then you met him. An eccentric older human — brilliant, odd, and completely unconcerned with your past. He took an interest in your skills and, over time, became something you hadn't had since Evermeet: a mentor. He taught you how to read the hidden architecture of magic — not just stone and mortar, but how arcane energy flows through the land itself. Leylines. Currents. The invisible blueprints beneath the surface of the world.

When he eventually moved on, he left you with immense skill and, for the first time in years, a rebuilt sense of stability. You never learned his full story. But you owe him more than you'll ever say.

### The Neverwinter Partnership

Eventually, you moved north to **Neverwinter**. You put your analytical mind to work in city enforcement — not as a soldier, but as the unit's cartographer and forensic analyst. You map crime scenes. You analyze magical residue. You read a room the way a surgeon reads a wound.

You found an unlikely family in your unit: **Kevori**, a no-nonsense investigator; **Delvin Fearnehart**, Kevori's partner; and a **Dragonborn** — the unit's volatile, sharp-tongued magical muscle. They don't pry into your past. You don't volunteer it. It works.

They are your stability now. And you protect stability the way a drowning man protects a piece of driftwood.

### What You Know

- You were born in Evermeet but exiled for breaching the island's wards with a device you built.
- A mysterious "muse" manipulated you into building it, and the realization that you were used — not brilliant — left a deep scar.
- You rebuilt your life in Athkatla under an eccentric human mentor who taught you to read the invisible flow of magic through the land.
- You are the analytical linchpin of a Neverwinter investigative unit alongside Kevori, Delvin Fearnehart, and a Dragonborn partner.
- You value your independence, your paranoia, and your walls. You will not be made a fool of again.

### What You Don't Know

You don't know who the "muse" truly was, or what she wanted with the breach. You don't know the real identity of the old human mentor who rebuilt you in Athkatla. And you don't know that the skills he taught you have a purpose far larger than mapping crime scenes.
